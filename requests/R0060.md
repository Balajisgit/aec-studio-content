# R60 · Fit to plot: the largest rectangle inside an irregular plot's setbacks, as the ground floor's outer walls

The owner: "create a pipelien for fitiing the building in a irregular plot with maximum rectangle leaving after setback. plan first". The owner's answers to the plan: the rectangle at **any angle, best area**; setbacks **front, rear and sides**; the plot **drawn on the canvas, read from coordinates or a survey file by an agent, or typed in the notes**; the result **the outer walls of the ground floor**.

## What it needs (app, aec-studio)

- **The site in the model.** `model.site = {boundary, front, setbacks: {front, rear, side}, edges?, name?}`: the corners in order (integer mm), the edge on the road, and the setbacks; `edges` gives an edge its own. The rear is the edge facing away from the front (its outward normal at more than 120° from the front's); every other edge is a side. Two operations, logged and undoable like any change: `set_site` (checked: three corners or more, no crossing edges, whole mm) and `clear_site`.
- **The geometry (kernel/site.js).** `maxRectangles(site, {top, maxCoverage, minSide})` turns the plot through every edge's direction and 0 to 87° in 3° steps, finds the largest buildable rectangle in each frame on a grid (with the best elsewhere, for an L-shaped plot), grows each side to 1 mm against the exact setbacks, then refines the best angles to a quarter degree. It keeps a 2 mm margin so the corners, rounded to whole mm, still pass `fitsSite`. Tested against a search every half degree: the same answer.
- **A Site tool on the canvas (P).** Click the corners and back on the first to close the plot; click an edge to make it the front; the setbacks and Clear the plot are in the options panel. The plan shows the plot, the front edge, each edge's kind and setback, and the setback line (dashed red); faint on upper floors.
- **Code stages.** `fit-max-rectangle` (the best and up to two others, with the plot's area and share) and `place-footprint` (a closed run of walls on the lowest level, its centreline inset by half the wall so the outer face sits on the rectangle). Code stages may now read and change their project's model (one logged step by the pipeline, in the run's edits).
- **A decision form, `pick-footprint`.** The plot, the setback line and the footprints as a small plan; pick one, optionally smaller about the same centre, the wall thickness (230 mm) and height (the floor's).
- **Checks.** `site-set`, `fits-within-setbacks`, `footprint-within-setbacks` (the picked rectangle and the walls' outer face).
- **A pipeline input may be optional** (`input.optional`): the project itself (its plot) is enough to start.

## What it needs (content)

- `pipelines/fit-to-plot/1.0.0.json`: Read the plot (the Site reader, only when the project has no plot or the brief brings files) → Fit the largest rectangle (code) → Pick the footprint (decision) → Draw the outer walls (code).
- `agents/site-reader.json`: reads typed coordinates, a coordinates file or a survey, and sets the plot with `project_edit` and `set_site`; says what it assumed (the front, default setbacks of 3 m front and rear, 1.5 m sides).
- The three check definitions.

## Later

- Setbacks from the local bylaws by plot size and road width (a knowledge file per firm).
- Shapes other than a rectangle (L, courtyard), and a height or FAR limit.
- A survey's real coordinates kept with an offset to the plan's origin.
