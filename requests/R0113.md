# R113 · The model from first principles: nodes and segments, spaces worked out, Objects on hosts, zones on rings

Discussed in session 41. The model is rebuilt from first principles: two primers, spaces worked out from them, and Objects that attach to primers or to other Objects. Replaces faces and all meanings (roles) on primers.

Owner: "only nodes and segments are primers - it forms the space - has custom name and use from a select"; "objects can attach to another object(s) - a segment attached with wall which in turns attached with a door or window and a slab. also we can solve the geometry operation too - for eg door - boolean into walls"; "zone is a segment type that dont form a true space - either it splits or groups".

1. Primers: nodes and segments only; faces are no longer stored.
2. Segments: what a line divides is its own property alone, whatever is attached to it: room line (forms spaces), zone line (ignored by spaces, bounds zones only), reference line (divides nothing: grids, setback lines, construction lines, survey edges). A new segment is a room line; it can be switched (owner: ok). A wall may stand on a zone line too, e.g. a glass wall inside a bathroom (owner: "a wall can be placed on a zone line too... so line define by it properties alone").
3. Spaces: each area the room lines close, never stored as a shape; a name and a use picked from the typology's list. A tag point inside keeps them: on a split the part with the tag keeps them; on a merge the bigger one's tag wins (owner: ok).
4. Objects attach to a host, a primer or another Object, and may attach to several: node -> column, furniture, fixture; segment -> wall, fence, railing; wall -> door, window (at a point along it, with a sill); a ring of walls and bare segments -> slab, one per floor plate, to the walls' outer faces, voids and stairwells as holes (owner: ok); the top floor's walls -> roof slab. Finishes are Objects too, attached to a wall or a floor Object (owner: "finish is an another object attached to a wall, floor object"); nothing attaches to a space.
5. Zones: a name and a use attached to a closed ring of segments (zone lines, and walls where it follows them). The Studio works out what it does: splits a space, groups whole spaces, or crosses part of a room with each part's area. Owner: ring "ok", part of a room "allow", overlapping zones "allow", zone lines thin and dashed, hidden with their layer "ok".
6. Geometry operations, declared by each Object on its host, worked out when drawing or exporting and never stored: cut (a door or window into its wall; a stair or void into its slab), join (walls at a corner), stop under (a wall under the slab above), rest on (walls on their floor's slab).
7. Hosting rules: a host moves, what it holds moves with it; no loops; a ring's segment split, the ring takes both pieces; removing a host takes what it holds with it, and the step lists what went (owner's words).
8. No meanings (owner: ok): an Object's kind (in the Library) says what it behaves as; its values are the Object's parameters per Placement; a firm's own is just an Object of that kind. Drawing a wall attaches a generic wall Object at once, swapped later; Needs only from pipelines matching a brief (owner: ok). A space's fields: name, use, number, and the extra values its typology's use asks (seats, beds) (owner: ok). Primers alone read as a plan: room lines solid, zone lines dashed, reference lines faint, space tags with name, use and area. The Library's meanings/ folder and assign_role go.
9. Site: positions are labels whose list comes from the use (a plot's: front, rear, side, own setback), assigned to segments (owner: "labels as per use type -varies - assigned"); a plot or footprint is a space with that use (owner: "yes").

Changes: the model's format moves to a new version, old models convert by themselves (each room face becomes a space with its name and use; a level's slab pieces join into one slab on its edge walls); templates, the test-fit solver, the canvas and the checks read the new model. Big: plan in steps, each its own pull request.
